About

What SciVigilance is

A daily signal on space and science. Built to replace "Science in 280" — not a summary blog, but a sharp, curated digest with a point of view.

The mission

SciVigilance exists to make the day's most important space and science stories understandable, not just readable. We're not trying to be a wire service or an AI summary feed. We're trying to be the thing you open once a day and trust to have done the work of reading, checking, and connecting the dots for you.

Every story in the digest is chosen for one of three reasons: it changes what we think we know, it moves a major mission forward, or it's genuinely fascinating in a way that deserves an audience. If none of those apply, it doesn't make the cut.

Where the content comes from

Our reporting draws on primary sources — mission agencies, observatories, laboratories, and the peer-reviewed record behind them. NASA, ESA, CERN, NOAA, and the universities and institutes that run the actual science are our backbone. When we report on a result, we try to get you to the result itself, not just to the press release.

The daily social track (Daily X) is a companion layer: curated, sourced highlights from the agencies and teams doing the work. It's where the day's signal first appears, before it's written up in the digest.

Understand, don't just scroll

Every story earns a reader. We explain the "why it matters," not just the "what happened."

02

Source the signal

Primary sources first. When we cite a result, we point you toward the result, not the rumor.

03

Be honest about uncertainty

Science is provisional. We say "this suggests," "this rules out," "this is still being verified" — and we mean it.

04

One signal a day

Restraint is the point. A single, sharp digest beats a firehose. Quality over volume, always.

A note on this site

This is a demo build. The articles you're reading are sample content seeded to show the layout, voice, and interaction — they are clearly labeled as such and are not live dispatches from a real publishing feed. The structure is production-ready: swap in a real data/latest.json pipeline and the same pages render real stories.

The images are real, sourced from public-domain and Creative-Commons collections (NASA, ESA, CERN, NOAA, Wikimedia Commons), and credited on every card and article.
